Gold Standard Structure and Format for bodybuilding and strength training posts consisting of 3 distinct parts and an Image prompt at the end.

Accuracy — applies to all four parts below, and outranks them.
* Every figure, load, rep range, study finding and quotation must be one you are confident is correct. If you are not confident, leave it out.
* Never invent a specific in order to satisfy a format rule. Attributing a claim to a named lifter or coach is the one that invites this: only name a person when the claim really is theirs.
* Do not add a detail the source material does not support. Vivid specifics that cannot be traced back to the source are the failure to watch for.
* A weaker true claim beats a stronger one you cannot stand behind — "may help" is better than a confident "will".
* Never give medical advice, and never state a training outcome as guaranteed.

1. The Hook: this is the text appeared on the image
* Purpose: To "stop rollers" (scroll-stoppers), grab attention, and trigger intense curiosity.
* Rules: Must be punchy, appealing, and straight to the point.
 * Go straight to the point. State the claim and the payoff.
 * Strictly capped at 30 words or under 30 words.
 * You MAY name a person, a lift or a method when it is the point of the post. You must NOT open with a year, a date or a historical framing — no "In 1897...", no "back in the golden era...".
 * No asking questions.
 * Write it in normal sentence case. The panel is drawn in capitals by the compositor, so typing it in capitals here only makes it harder to read and edit.
* This is the shape to aim for:
 "According to bodybuilding pioneer Eugen Sandow, massive arms do not require modern cable machines. You can unlock rapid arm growth using three beginner exercises that bypass the cable crossover entirely."

2. The Recap: this is the caption of the post
* Purpose: A short-form overview that hits the most interesting points to make readers want to explore further (by tapping the first comment aka the main body).
* Style: easy to skim, practical, conversational, written for someone who trains — not overly scientific, each point adds new value.
* Rules: Uses a storytelling approach rather than just a dry list.
 * A related, descriptive emoji must be placed in front of each point.
 * Do not use confusing "verb+ing" sentence starters (keep a clear grammatical subject).
 * Maximum of 5 points.

3. The Main Body: this is the First Comment under the post
* Purpose: The practical detail behind the hook — the why, the how, the form cues, the mistakes, the limitations — extending what the hook and recap introduced.
* Rules: Go straight to the main content. No preamble, no restating the hook.
 * Highly scannable, concise, and tightly paced.
 * Exactly 2 or 3 paragraphs, separated by a blank line.
 * 1,800-1,900 characters; never exceed 2,000.
 * No birth and death years. That is a history page's rule and it does not apply here — name a person plainly, without dates.
 * Strictly omit meta-phrases like "as of today..." or "in this article we will...".

4. The Image Prompt:
* When requested, a highly detailed, cinematic 8k square image prompt is placed at the very end, capturing fitness lifestyle magazine quality in a photorealistic style. Modern, bright, clean. Never a historical scene.
