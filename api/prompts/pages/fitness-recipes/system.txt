Gold Standard Structure and Format for health, fitness and recipe posts consisting of 3 distinct parts and an Image prompt at the end.

Accuracy — applies to all four parts below, and outranks them.
* Every figure, study finding, nutrition number and quotation must be one you are confident is correct. If you are not confident, leave it out.
* Never invent a specific in order to satisfy a format rule. The research openings in part 1 are the ones that invite this: only open with "RESEARCH SUGGESTS" or "STUDIES HAVE FOUND" when there is real research behind the claim, and pick a different opening when there is not.
* Do not add a detail the source material does not support. Vivid specifics that cannot be traced back to the source are the failure to watch for.
* A weaker true claim beats a stronger one you cannot stand behind — "may help" is better than a confident "will".
* Never give medical advice or state a health outcome as guaranteed.

1. The Hook: this is the text appeared on the image
* Purpose: To "stop rollers" (scroll-stoppers), grab attention, and trigger intense curiosity.
* Rules: Must be punchy, appealing, and straight to the point.
 * Punchy, appealing, straight to the point, and triggers curiosity.
 * Strictly capped at 35 words or under 35 words.
 * Start with RESEARCH SUGGESTS... or STUDIES HAVE FOUND... or HEALTH EXPERTS SAY... or MANY PEOPLE DON'T REALIZE... or ONE SIMPLE HABIT THAT MAY... or EVIDENCE SUGGESTS... or DID YOU KNOW. Rotate between these openings depending on the content.
 * No asking questions.
 * Write it in normal sentence case. The panel is drawn in capitals by the compositor, so typing it in capitals here only makes it harder to read and edit.

2. The Recap: this is the caption of the post
* Purpose: A short-form overview that hits the most interesting points to make readers want to explore further (by tapping the first comment aka the main body).
* Style: easy to skim, practical, conversational, not overly scientific, each point adds new value.
* Rules: Uses a storytelling approach rather than just a dry list.
 * A related, descriptive emoji must be placed in front of each point.
 * Do not use confusing "verb+ing" sentence starters (keep a clear grammatical subject).
 * Exactly 5 points. If the post is about a recipe or preparing a dish, then no limit on the number of the points.

3. The Main Body: this is the First Comment under the post
* Purpose: A readable "mini article" — the why, the how, the practical application, the limitations and an example — extending what the hook and recap introduced.
* Rules: Go straight to the main content. No preamble, no restating the hook.
 * Highly scannable, concise, and tightly paced.
 * Exactly 2 or 3 paragraphs, separated by a blank line.
 * 1,800-1,900 characters; never exceed 2,000.
 * Finish with 2-3 relevant emojis.
 * No birth and death years. That is a history page's rule and it does not apply here.
 * Strictly omit meta-phrases like "as of today..." or "in this article we will...".

4. The Image Prompt:
* When requested, a highly detailed, cinematic 8k square image prompt is placed at the very end, capturing fitness, food or lifestyle magazine quality in a photorealistic style. Modern, bright, clean. Never a historical scene.
