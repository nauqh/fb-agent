## Where they live

The old tool stores prompts as **columns on a per-Page row**, edited from the
Settings tab (`facebook-prompts-settings-panel.tsx`). Six text columns matter:
`system_prompt` (writer), `summarize_system_prompt`, `text_overlay_prompt` and
`image_gen_system_prompt`, plus `*_full_overlay` variants for its Template 2,
which this app does not have.

A null column is **not** "no prompt" — the panel substitutes a code default
(`templateToEdits`, `:141-150`). So a Page can look configured on screen and be
storing nothing.

## Fitness Recipes — `system_prompt` (writer), verbatim

```
Gold Standard Structure and Format for health, fitness, recipe posts consisting of 3 distinct parts and an Image prompt at the end.

1. The Hook: this is the text appeared on the image
* Purpose: To "stop rollers" (scroll-stoppers), grab attention, and trigger intense curiosity.
* Rules: Must be punchy, appealing, and straight to the point.
 * Punchy, appealing, straight to the point, and triggers curiosity.
 * Strictly capped at 35 words or under 35 words. Written in all CAPITAL LETTERS.
 * Start with RESEARCH SUGGESTS... or STUDIES HAVE FOUND... or HEALTH EXPERTS SAY... or MANY PEOPLE DON'T REALIZE... or ONE SIMPLE HABIT THAT MAY... or EVIDENCE SUGGESTS...or DID YOU KNOW. Rotate between these openings depending on the content.

2. The Recap: this is the caption of the post
* Purpose: A short-form overview that hits the most interesting points to make readers want to explore further (by tapping the first comment aka the main body).
* Style: easy to skim, practical, conversational, not overly scientific, each point adds new value.
* Rules: Uses a storytelling approach rather than just a dry chronological list of events.
 * A related, descriptive emoji must be placed in front of each point.
 * Do not use confusing "verb+ing" sentence starters (keep a clear grammatical subject).
 * Exactly 5 points. If the post is about a recipe or preparing a dish, then no limit on the number of the points.

3. The Main Body: this is the First Comment under the post
* Purpose: The complete, in-depth readable research as "mini article", explaining the "why, how, practical application, limitations, examples, etc." that extends the details introduced in the hook and recap, giving readers the full picture.
* Rules: Highly scannable, concise, and tightly paced (ideally between 800 to 1,300 characters max or 200–300 words). Finish with 2–3 relevant emojis.

4. The Image Prompt:
* When requested, a highly detailed, cinematic 8k square image prompt is placed at the very end to visually capture the fitness or food or lifestyle magazine quality in a photorealistic style.
```

## Fitness Recipes — `text_overlay_prompt`, verbatim

```
Image text panel (composited in post-processing — not drawn by the image model):

1. textOverlay (same as hook unless instructed otherwise):
* Scroll-stopping paragraph on a solid black panel at the bottom of the post image.
* Start with RESEARCH SUGGESTS... or STUDIES HAVE FOUND... or HEALTH EXPERTS SAY... or MANY PEOPLE DON'T REALIZE... or ONE SIMPLE HABIT THAT MAY... or EVIDENCE SUGGESTS...or DID YOU KNOW. Rotate between these openings depending on the content.
* Strictly capped at 35 words or under 35 words.

2. highlightPhrases (gold highlights on the text panel):
* Return 1-3 exact substrings copied verbatim from textOverlay (never more than 3).
* Use short phrases (2-8 words each): numbers with units, names of the exercise or movement or ingredient or action, time, health benefits — e.g. "3 times", "15 minutes before bed", "pairing green tea with ginger", "deep yoga squat", "sleeping with a pillow", "stronger joints" as separate entries.

Visual rendering (added in code — for your awareness only):
* Black panel, white body text, orange (#f96030) on highlightPhrases, League Spartan font, centered.

Final post card assembly (layers 2–3 are added in code — do NOT draw them):
1. HERO PHOTO (~75% height) — YOU generate only this: full-bleed photorealistic fitness or food or lifestyle magazine photograph, square output, no UI, no text.
2. BLACK TEXT BOX (up to ~25% height at bottom, sized to fit copy) — solid black panel with white/orange copy (added in code).
3. CIRCLE BRAND LOGO — uploaded brand logo, top-right corner of the hero (added in code). Leave top-right empty in your photo.

Your output must be layer 1 only — a clean photograph with zero text and zero logos.
```

Everything from *"Final post card assembly"* down is the stale geometry
`writer/prompts.py` predicted: a 75%-height hero and a circular logo neither
compositor draws. `layout.yml` and the `{panel_pct}` token are the geometry
here. Take the voice and the subject rules; leave the layers behind.

## What this answers, and where it contradicts the 2026-08-15 message

The prompts predate the feedback (July; the message is 15 August). Where the two
disagree, **the message is newer and wins** — but the gap is worth showing the
client, because in three places they asked us for a number they had already
written down differently.

| Ask (2026-08-15) | Fitness Recipes prompt (July) | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Hook "capped at 30 words" | "capped at 35 words or under 35 words" | Client's own example is **exactly 30**. Ask which. |
| Overlay in ALL CAPS (C4) | "Written in all CAPITAL LETTERS" — writer rule | Already asked for, in the *prompt*. Shipped as a **drawing** setting instead, which is why FR's drafts were not in caps: this line asks the model, and the model drifts. |
| First comment "capped at 1,500 characters" | "between 800 to 1,300 characters max or 200–300 words" | Two different caps. 800 is also the floor question (F5 → Q5) answered: they do have one in mind. |
| First comment "3-4 short paragraphs" | not stated | New in the message. |
| Hero "modern, bright, fitness lifestyle magazine" | "fitness or food or lifestyle magazine quality"; "photorealistic fitness or food or lifestyle magazine photograph" | Consistent. And it answers Q2: their own wording covers **both** Pages, food included. |
| Overlay "without listing historical year/event/character" | FR simply omits the rule; BBTT still carries it verbatim | Confirms the fix is deletion of HR's clause, not new prose. |

Two more things the prompts settle that nobody asked:

- **Highlight count.** FR says **1–3** phrases of 2–8 words; ours says 5–8 of
  1–4 (`overlay.txt:8`). The comprehension note flagged 5–8 gold phrases over a
  30-word hook as a coming complaint. The client already decided: fewer, longer.
- **Birth/death years.** FR's prompt has no such rule; BBTT's has History
  Retraced's. Confirms Q6 in the direction expected — off for both.
- **Orange, not gold.** FR's prompt names `#f96030`, which is already the
  `highlight_color` override on the BBTT row in this app. The colour has landed;
  the prompt is where it was written down.

## Bearing on F5's actual decision

The question F5 posed — do prompts become per-Page **files** we edit, per-Page
**rows** the client edits, or numbers-in-a-row plus prose-in-a-file — is not
changed by what was read, and option 3 is still the recommendation. But the
evidence is now stronger in one specific way:

`writer/prompts.py` justified the move to files by measuring 2,030 of 2,350
characters as byte-identical across three pages. **This read reproduces that
result on the current data**: BBTT is 100% identical to History Retraced across
all four prompt columns, and has been since 2026-07-05. The editable textarea did
not give the client a per-Page prompt for BBTT. It gave them a copy of somebody
else's that they believed was theirs — for six weeks, until the drafts came out
wrong and they told us so.

That is the argument against option 2, made by the client's own data rather than
by us.
